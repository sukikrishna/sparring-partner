# Beta: detained surgical staff

Health for All (HfA), an international medical NGO, has run a surgical unit at a rural district hospital in Country Beta since ethnic clashes in 2020. It has now decided, with little local consultation, to close that unit and move the team to a war zone elsewhere. Local armed tribesmen then confined nine international staff to their residence, and the hospital has stopped working. A delegation from HfA headquarters is about to negotiate with the tribal leaders.

## If you are the HfA delegation

**Your situation.** You have flown in from Geneva to secure the safe release of nine Swiss surgical staff held in their residence in Iguwafe. HfA has a strict no-ransom policy, its main donors (Germany and Switzerland) want the team redeployed, and headquarters considers a long-term presence in Iguwafe not viable. Your goal is to get your colleagues out safely and settle what happens next at the hospital.

**Across the table.** The Iguwafe tribal council, whose leader has already set out conditions and ended your first meeting without letting you respond.

**Start with these documents**

1. **Case background** (`hfa-case-00`). The timeline and the tribal leaders' first concerns.
2. **First meeting with the Tribal Leaders** (`hfa-case-16`). The council's stated demands and the ground rules it is setting for you.
3. **Meeting with the head surgeon of HfA in Iguwafe, accompanied by two armed tribesmen** (`hfa-case-12`). The only first-hand account of life in the residence and of how the detained team feels.
4. **Minutes of Meeting with the German Ambassador** (`hfa-case-05`). How one main donor frames the crisis and what she can and cannot authorise.
5. **Minutes of Meeting with the Swiss Ambassador** (`hfa-case-06`). A second donor view, with some different signals on flexibility and support.

**Questions to keep in mind**

- HfA calls this a labor dispute, the UN and press talk of hostages, and the hospital director calls the staff "guests". What does each label commit you to?
- The two ambassadors do not say quite the same things about funding, timing and what Beta needs. Where exactly do their positions diverge?
- Accounts of the staff's freedom of movement and safety differ between the head surgeon, the police chief and your own encounter at the residence. Which account do you rely on, and why?
- The tribal leader's demands mix hospital operations, compensation for guards' families and the measles outbreak. Who else in your documents raises each of these issues, and with what interests?
- Several local actors (the district administrator, the police, the guards) describe the tribal leaders' role differently. What does that tell you about who actually decides?

## If you are the Tribal council

**Your situation.** You speak for the tribal leadership of Beta Town. Your people have confined nine HfA surgeons to their residence after HfA announced, without consulting you, that it would close the surgical unit at the district hospital. Your documents include press coverage, social posts and informant reports on HfA's meetings. Your goal is to secure an outcome for your community before the surgeons leave.

**Across the table.** The HfA delegation sent from Geneva.

**Start with these documents**

1. **Public post on the HfA website** (`hfa-website`). HfA's own public account of the transfer and the closure.
2. **Statement from the UN Humanitarian Coordinator** (`un-statement`). What HfA reportedly said privately about its funding, its donors and its no-ransom policy.
3. **HfA meeting with local guards and families** (`guards-meeting`). The widows' claims and what the delegation committed to.
4. **HfA meeting with the district administration head** (`district-meeting`). A local official's view of your role, which may not match your own.
5. **News article on Beta (Swiss source)** (`beta-news-ch`). How the detention is being told to the surgeons' home audience.

**Questions to keep in mind**

- HfA's public post and the informant reports give different reasons for leaving. How do the public and private explanations fit together, if at all?
- The Swiss news article and the head surgeon's child describe events and timelines in ways that differ from other accounts. What details differ, and what effect might that framing have abroad?
- The UN statement mentions possible ransom demands. How does that rumour shape how HfA and outside governments see you?
- The district administrator and the police chief both talk about the tribal leaders' role, but in different terms. Where do they support you, and where do they not?
- The hospital director describes the guards as falling under your authority while raising questions about their past affiliations. How does that affect how others read your demands?
