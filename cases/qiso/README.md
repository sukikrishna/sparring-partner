# Qiso: refugees at the border town

Several thousand people have fled the conflict in Chiasa into Qiso, a small farming town on Sitwan's eastern border. Many of them come from Kiman, whose families have worked Qiso's almond and pecan harvest for generations. Sitwan's Border Authority wants every Chiasan in Qiso gathered in the municipal stadium and transferred to Refugee Detention Facilities (RDFs) within two weeks, and has sent an officer to agree a plan with the Mayor.

## If you are the Mayor of Qiso

**Your situation.** You run a town that is already sheltering hundreds of people in its school gym, temple courtyard and truck parking lot, with many more in church halls, private homes and orchards. The national government has told you to use your police to gather all of them in the stadium. Your residents, farmers, churches, school, police and finance department have written to you, and they disagree. Your goal is to reach an arrangement with the Border Authority that your town can actually carry out and live with.

**Across the table.** An officer of the Border Authority, who arrives this week to agree a timeline with you.

**Start with these documents**

1. **Official letter from the Border Authority to the Mayor** (`ba-letter`). The formal request, and the list of things you are asked to bring to the meeting.
2. **Refugee Processing and Transport Protocols** (`protocols`). The rules the request rests on.
3. **Fiscal implications of the stadium request** (`finance-memo`). What the stadium plan would cost, how compensation is paid, and how much of the town's budget depends on the harvest.
4. **Police staffing and public order** (`police-memo`). What your police can and cannot do, and what is actually behind the rise in calls.
5. **Letter from the Elders of Kiman** (`kiman-elders`). The view from the community you are being asked to move, including an offer.

**Questions to keep in mind**

- How many people are really in Qiso, and where are they? Compare the finance memo, police memo, church letter and online figures.
- The protocols treat harvest workers with valid permits differently from new arrivals, and say something specific about "emergency assistance" versus "long-term shelter". How do those distinctions line up with what your churches and farmers describe?
- Several incidents (the schoolboy, the market, the temple courtyard) are reported by more than one source. Which accounts agree, which conflict, and which are second-hand?
- The Border Authority and Emergency International both ask for lists of names and locations. What would a list be used for, and by whom?
- You hold information shared with you privately. What would change if it became known, and to whom?

## If you are the Border Authority officer

**Your situation.** You have been deployed to Qiso by headquarters to register the Chiasans in the town and move them to RDFs within two weeks. You carry both your role card and restricted instructions that you are told not to share with municipal officials. Your goal is to complete the task within the law and the time you have been given.

**Across the table.** The Mayor of Qiso, whose police and facilities your instructions assume you can use.

**Start with these documents**

1. **Role card: Officer of the Border Authority** (`officer-rolecard`). Your background, your view of the stakes, and your redlines.
2. **Deployment to Qiso (restricted instructions)** (`hq-instructions`). What headquarters expects, what it believes about numbers, security and health, and what you may offer.
3. **Official letter from the Border Authority to the Mayor** (`ba-letter`). What the Mayor has already been told and asked to prepare.
4. **Refugee Processing and Transport Protocols** (`protocols`). The legal basis for the operation, including compensation, transport and private sheltering.
5. **How a train attack in Chiasa set off a refugee crisis** (`intl-news-4`). The background to the conflict and a public dispute over the national arrival figures.

**Questions to keep in mind**

- Headquarters, the municipality, NGOs and social media all give different numbers. How were each of them arrived at, and how do they compare with the RDF capacity described in the shared documents?
- Your instructions describe security and disease risks. Which shared documents support those claims, which complicate them, and how strong is each source?
- The protocols treat seasonal harvest workers and new arrivals differently. Who in Qiso falls into which group, and how would anyone know?
- Your instructions rely on local police and the stadium. What do the shared documents suggest about whether those are available, and what the Mayor may be hearing from residents?
- Some of what you know is restricted. How does that limit what you can explain, and what you can ask the Mayor to accept?
